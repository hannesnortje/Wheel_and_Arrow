5

The inner foundation

What wears down our enthusiasm

Children are curious, flexible and unafraid of failing. Three things wear that away.

The weight of daily dutiesThe busier we are, the shorter our view.
The pull to fit inThe more we bend to be accepted, the more our creativity fades.
A learned fear of failureHarshly treated mistakes teach us to play it safe.

Part of personal leadership is to recover the openness and hope you once had as a child.

6

The inner foundation

A healthier self-image

We tend to live in line with the person we believe we are.

See that you are uniqueThere is no one you need to measure yourself against.
Accept yourself honestlyName strengths and weaknesses plainly, as facts.
Speak true words to yourselfBelievable statements push back old doubts.
Reach small goalsEach success shows what you are capable of.

And make peace with mistakes – they are steps to build on, not stones to trip over.

8

The Wheel of Life

Score your wheel – an honest snapshot

1 Score each area 1–101 at the centre = not satisfied at all; 10 at the rim = completely satisfied.
2 Mark each spokeMeasured against the standard you have set for yourself.
3 Join the marksThe outline is the shape of your wheel today.

A wheel with a 9 for work and a 2 for health gives a bumpy ride. Let it grow outward – and stay round.

The mechanism of goal setting

Why write it down?

✕ The famous "3% Yale study" never took place.

Gail Matthews (Dominican University, 2007): 149 adults, five groups, four weeks – average progress per group.

Only thought about goals4.28
Wrote goals down5.08
+ action steps6.08
+ shared with a friend6.41
+ weekly progress report7.6

Write it down · commit to steps · be accountable to someone.

12

The mechanism of goal setting

Shape every goal to be SMART

SSpecificNot "save money" but "I am building an emergency fund."
MMeasurableNumbers or milestones you can track.
AAttainableIt stretches you but stays realistic.
RRelevant & RealisticIt fits your values and this season of life.
TTime-boundA goal without a date is a wish.
13

The Goal Planning Worksheet

The worksheet, section by section

The goalA SMART sentence in the positive present tense
Why it mattersWhat you gain – and what you avoid
Obstacles & plansName the resistance; plan each detour
Action stepsSmall steps, each with a date
Who should knowPeople who support you and ask
Track progressWhen and how you check
Worth it?Values and cost – a last check
Reminders & picturesCues that keep the goal in view
14

A completed example

A finished worksheet: Work & Finances

The goal“I am saving 1,500 USD in a dedicated emergency fund by automating a 500 USD transfer on the 1st of October, November and December.”
What I gainPeace of mind, less stress in my marriage, and protection for my family against unexpected costs without relying on credit cards.
What I avoidThe anxiety of living from pay cheque to pay cheque, the pain of high-interest debt, and arguments about money at home.
Impulse spending at the weekend.→ A fixed cash-only budget for weekends; the cards stay at home.
Forgetting to make the transfer.→ An automatic recurring transfer on payday in the banking app.
